## Session objective

Design a bounded professional workflow in which AI has a defined task, approved inputs, an
inspectable output, an explicit verification checkpoint, visible human responsibility, reusable
process components, and a measurable reason to be included.

## Facilitation principles

1. Map the professional process before discussing the prompt.
2. Define the desired result before selecting an AI use case.
3. Give AI bounded operations rather than undefined professional responsibility.
4. Keep source-grounding and verification practices from S04 active throughout the workflow.
5. Make human responsibility visible at the appropriate checkpoint.
6. Do not imply that every human checkpoint requires formal approval.
7. Compare manual and AI-assisted processes without assuming that AI is better.
8. Use the same or sufficiently comparable task conditions when making before-and-after claims.
9. Keep the Soubré package fictional and do not invent operational rules.
10. Treat workflow improvement as a response to an observed failure or measurable need.

## Slide 04 — One prompt is not a workflow

- **Identifier:** S05-04
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Weak model:

TASK → PROMPT → ANSWER

Professional model:

INPUT → AI TASK → CHECK → HUMAN ACTION → OUTPUT

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A useful model response does not by itself define a reliable process.

A prompt describes an interaction with the model. A workflow also identifies inputs, preparation,
verification, exception handling, human action, and what happens to the result.

## Slide 07 — Map the process before changing it

Manual baseline:

### Teaching Notes

## Slide 08 — Find the bounded AI step

- **Identifier:** S05-08
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Good candidate:

STRUCTURE A DRAFT BRIEF

Not:

RUN OPERATIONS · MAKE OFFICIAL DECISIONS

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** AI should receive a specific operation, not an undefined professional role.

The bounded step should have recognizable inputs and an output that a person can inspect. Keep
official organizational authority outside the AI step.

## Slide 09 — Give AI a bounded task

- **Identifier:** S05-09
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

INPUT → SPECIFIC OPERATION → EXPECTED OUTPUT

Possible operations:

- summarize / extract
- classify
- compare
- flag
- draft
- reformat

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A bounded task makes the AI step testable.

Connect to earlier sessions without reteaching the capabilities. The workflow designer should be
able to state exactly what information enters, what operation occurs, and what kind of output should
emerge.

## Slide 10 — Inputs must be known and allowed

## Slide 12 — Steps, conditions, and checkpoints

TASK → STEPS → CONDITIONS → CHECKPOINTS → RESULT

Example:

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A workflow includes what happens when conditions are not satisfied.

## Slide 13 — Meet the Soubré workflow package

- **Identifier:** S05-13
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Source A — CP-A · 5 October 2026 · intake paused 09:10 · resumed 09:40 · reason not supplied
- Source B — SB-201 · 540 kg
- Source B — SB-202 · weight missing · needs completion
- Source C — SB-201 → Area A at 12:15
- Source D — SB-202 requires staff review

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Every workflow decision must stay inside the fictional source package.

Do not infer why intake paused. Do not assign a quality, approval, payment, certification, ownership,
or commercial meaning to any lot. `Area A` is only a recorded destination label.

## Slide 14 — Establish the manual baseline

- **Identifier:** S05-14
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Manual preparation requires a person to:

1. read the approved sources;
2. identify relevant facts and gaps;
3. draft the brief;
4. verify it against the sources;
5. use or send the reviewed result.

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Baseline mapping makes later comparison possible.

Ask learners what can actually be observed about this process. Several sources must be consulted,
facts must be selected, and the result must be checked. Do not invent a time estimate or claim that
the process is slow.

## Slide 15 — Insert AI into one step

- **Identifier:** S05-15
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

APPROVED INPUTS → CHECK COMPLETENESS → AI DRAFT → VERIFY → HUMAN ACTION → USE

AI task:

Structure the supplied information into a short operations brief.

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** AI assists one defined part of the process.

State the source constraint: use only supplied information, preserve missing information as missing,
and do not infer causes, approvals, quality results, payments, or other unsupported facts.

## Slide 16 — Put the human checkpoint in the workflow

- **Identifier:** S05-16
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

A checkpoint answers:

WHAT? · AGAINST WHAT? · WHO? · WHAT IF IT FAILS?

For this case:

- Check: draft facts
- Against: Sources A–D
- Who: staff reviewer
- Failure: correct or escalate

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** “Human review” becomes useful only when the checkpoint is operationally clear.

Formal approval is not automatically required. The appropriate human action depends on the task.
Here, the reviewer checks whether the draft preserves the fictional source facts.

## Slide 17 — Test the AI-assisted step

- **Identifier:** S05-17
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Deliberately flawed draft:

Intake at CP-A was interrupted by a power outage and resumed at 09:40. Lot SB-201 weighed 540 kg
and was approved for storage in Area A. Lot SB-202 is awaiting completion because its weight is
missing.

Classify:

- supported;
- unsupported;
- requires careful wording.

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A workflow must detect failures that a fluent output can hide.

Learners should identify `power outage` and `approved for storage` as unsupported. `Area A` is a
recorded destination, but the source does not establish what the label means.

## Slide 18 — Design the exception path

- **Identifier:** S05-18
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

CHECK PASSES? · ├── YES → CONTINUE · └── NO → CORRECT / ESCALATE

Also:

INPUT COMPLETE? · ├── YES → CONTINUE · └── NO → MARK GAP / REVIEW

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A professional workflow defines what happens when the expected path fails.

Do not treat correction and escalation as interchangeable. A supported correction may be possible;
an unresolved fact may require a responsible person or another approved source.

## Slide 19 — Make the process reusable

- **Identifier:** S05-19
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Reusable components:

- prompt template;
- required-input checklist;
- verification checklist;
- fixed output structure;
- exception rule.

Projected message:

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Reuse means preserving useful control points, not blindly repeating old content.

The reusable object is the method for handling a new source package. Inputs and factual content may
change while the controlled process remains stable.

## Slide 20 — Measure whether it helps

- **Identifier:** S05-20
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Possible measures:

- time / effort
- corrections / accuracy
- unsupported claims / risk
- consistency / reuse

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** AI creates professional value only if the workflow improves something that matters.

At least one baseline observation and one AI-assisted observation should be recorded when the
exercise permits. Use the same task or sufficiently comparable conditions when making a direct
before-and-after claim.

## Slide 21 — Faster does not automatically mean better

- **Identifier:** S05-21
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

WORKFLOW A · 5 minutes · 0 unsupported claims · WORKFLOW B · 3 minutes · 3 unsupported claims

Question:

Which workflow is better?

Answer:

Time alone is not enough to decide.

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The metric must reflect the professional result, not only speed.

This is a teaching example, not measured evidence about a real tool. A workflow may be faster while
requiring more corrections or introducing unacceptable factual risk.

## Slide 22 — Improve from an observed failure

- **Identifier:** S05-22
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

DESIGN → TRY → CHECK → MEASURE → ADJUST

Example:

Observed: · unsupported cause invented · Adjustment: · strengthen source constraint · + · check unsupported causes

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Workflow improvement should respond to a specific observed failure.

Avoid vague requests to “make the AI better.” Identify the failure, modify the relevant process
control, and test again.

## Slide 23 — Professional transfer

- **Identifier:** S05-23
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Meeting follow-up:

approved notes → AI drafts actions → meeting owner verifies → follow-up is sent

Project status:

confirmed status → AI structures draft → project owner verifies → update is published

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The workflow method transfers beyond the cocoa example.

The professional domain changes, but the design questions remain: what is the result, what inputs
are authorized, what bounded task does AI perform, who checks it, and how is usefulness observed?

## Slide 25 — The professional habit

- **Identifier:** S05-25
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

Do not ask only:

Can AI do this?

Ask instead:

Where should AI fit?
Who checks the result?
Does the workflow actually help?

Program framework:

RESULT → CONTEXT → INSTRUCTION → SAFETY → VERIFICATION

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** AI should have a defined place, task, check, and measurable reason to be in the
process.
