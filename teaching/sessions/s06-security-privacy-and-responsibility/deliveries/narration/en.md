Responsible use of artificial intelligence starts before a prompt is written. A draft can sound clear and still be wrong, unauthorized, or sent to someone who should not receive it. So pause and ask three different questions: is the statement supported, is the information permitted for this tool and purpose, and who has authority to approve the next step?

Keep those questions separate. Checking a source does not grant permission to share its contents. Permission to prepare an internal brief does not authorize an outside recipient. A reviewer can correct a draft without having the authority to approve a payment, a storage decision, or another operational action.

The following example is a fictional classroom exercise, not a real cooperative record. Source A says that intake at collection point C P A paused at nine ten on October eighteenth, twenty twenty-six, and resumed at nine forty. It does not say why the pause occurred. Preserve that gap; do not invent a cause.

Source B records five hundred forty kilograms for lot S B six oh one. It says the weight for S B six oh two is missing and needs staff review. It does not identify who caused the missing field. A missing value is a reason to check the record, not evidence of blame.

Source C records a movement for S B six oh one at twelve fifteen, with Area A as the destination label. The source does not approve the lot, certify its quality, authorize storage, or make a payment decision. A destination is not an approval.

Now consider the three checks. First, compare every important statement with the allowed sources and keep their limits. Second, use only information permitted for the stated task and tool. In this exercise, the restricted attachment is excluded in full. Replacing a name would not make the remaining details safe or authorized.

Third, identify the person responsible for each decision. The designated reviewer checks the draft against the permitted sources, records corrections, and can return or stop it. The operations lead decides whether the brief may circulate to the internal operations team. That approval does not decide what happens to a lot, its storage, a payment, or a person.

Before sending to a new or external recipient, obtain a separate explicit decision. If permission is unclear, stop and ask the information owner. If restricted information has already been shared, stop further circulation and notify that owner; do not assume that deleting a copy reverses the exposure.

The lesson extends beyond one brief. Privacy, fairness, transparency, accountability, reliability, and human oversight require attention throughout an artificial intelligence workflow. Define responsibilities, check evidence and impacts, keep a route for correction, and monitor for new risks. A fluent answer is not proof, and one successful review is not a permanent guarantee.
