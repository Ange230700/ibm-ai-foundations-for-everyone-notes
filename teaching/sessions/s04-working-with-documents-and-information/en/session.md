## Session objective

Summarize, extract, compare, and analyze supplied information without converting gaps, conflicts, or inferences into facts, then verify important claims before professional use.

## Facilitation principles

1. Keep the approved source visible when learners evaluate a generated answer.
2. Use the five evidence states consistently: confirmed, missing, conflicting, derived, and unsupported.
3. Preserve the difference between a source gap and a source conflict.
4. Do not resolve the SB-104 weight discrepancy without new approved evidence.
5. Keep Source C's missing weight missing; use only the supplied incomplete-record rule.
6. Distinguish source facts, derived observations, and recommendations.
7. Use fictional, anonymized, public, or explicitly authorized information in practice.
8. Keep official decisions with authorized people and require human review before professional use.

- Unsupported details stay outside the factual answer

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The source determines what can be presented as supplied fact.

Explain that summarizing, reorganizing, and comparing may change form without creating new source facts. If a useful interpretation goes beyond direct source content, label it as derived or as a recommendation rather than pretending the source stated it.

## Slide 06 — Five evidence states

- **Identifier:** S04-06
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Confirmed
- Missing
- Conflicting
- Derived
- Unsupported

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Give information a visible status before using it.

Define the five categories: confirmed means explicitly supported; missing means not supplied; conflicting means supplied sources disagree; derived means calculated or inferred from supplied information; unsupported means no approved source supports the statement. Keep these labels available throughout the session.

## Slide 08 — Meet the Soubré source package

- **Identifier:** S04-08
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Source A: SB-104 · 3 Oct 2026 · 618 kg
- Source B: SB-104 · 3 Oct 2026 · 681 kg
- Source C: SB-105 · 3 Oct 2026 · weight missing
- Fictional records; no extra business rule is supplied

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The exercise package is deliberately small enough to verify line by line.

State the standing fictional rule for incomplete records: staff record lot ID, date, and weight; if a field is missing, mark the record “needs completion,” then have a staff member review it. No rule explains the conflicting weights in A and B.

## Slide 09 — Summarize Source A

- **Identifier:** S04-09
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Lot ID: SB-104
- Date: 3 October 2026
- Weight: 618 kg
- Add nothing that the source does not state

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A summary reduces information; it does not complete the source.

Ask for one short paragraph using only Source A. A valid summary may combine the three facts into prose. Reject additions about approval, quality, payment, ownership, certification, or any other status because Source A does not contain them.

## Slide 10 — Check the summary against the source

- **Identifier:** S04-10
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Identify each important claim
- Point to the source that supports it
- Remove or relabel anything unsupported

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Verification becomes concrete when each important claim has a source.

Ask “Where did this fact come from?” for the lot ID, date, and weight. This is simple claim-to-source tracing, not a requirement for formal citation software. The purpose is to make support inspectable before professional use.

## Slide 11 — Extract rather than summarize

- **Identifier:** S04-11
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Return the lot ID
- Return the date
- Return the weight

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Extraction retrieves defined fields instead of rewriting the whole source.

Contrast the task with summarization. A structured extraction is useful when the user already knows which fields matter. Exact values still need checking because a neat table or list can contain an extraction error.

## Slide 12 — Missing means missing

- **Identifier:** S04-12
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- SB-105 · 3 October 2026
- Weight: Missing
- Status: Needs completion
- Next step: Staff review

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Missing information must remain visibly missing.

The status and next step come from the supplied incomplete-record rule, not from a guessed workflow. Do not invent a weight, threshold, deadline, certification condition, payment state, or lot-acceptance decision. Missing information is not permission to guess.

## Slide 13 — Compare Source A and Source B

- **Identifier:** S04-13
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Lot ID: confirmed and consistent
- Date: confirmed and consistent
- Weight: conflicting

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Comparison should preserve both agreements and disagreements.

Have learners inspect the same fields in both records. The lot ID and date agree. The weight does not. Do not turn the comparison into a decision about which record is authoritative because the source package provides no basis for that decision.

## Slide 14 — A conflict must stay visible

- **Identifier:** S04-14
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Show 618 kg and 681 kg
- Do not choose without evidence
- No supplied source explains the difference
- An approved source or responsible person must resolve it

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A conflict is information about the evidence, not an invitation to hide one value.

Contrast “missing” with “conflicting.” Here there are two supplied values. Both remain visible until additional approved evidence or a responsible person resolves the discrepancy. Human review identifies responsibility; it does not authorize invention.

## Slide 15 — Gap versus conflict

- **Identifier:** S04-15
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Missing: the source provides no value
- Conflicting: supplied sources provide different values
- Describe the evidence problem correctly
- Neither case permits a guess

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Correctly naming the evidence problem changes the next professional action.

For a missing field, the supplied incomplete-record rule gives a status and staff review. For the conflicting weights, no resolution procedure is supplied. Keep that difference explicit rather than applying the missing-field rule to a conflict.

## Slide 16 — Fact, derived observation, recommendation

- **Identifier:** S04-16
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Fact: Source A records 618 kg
- Derived: the supplied records disagree on weight
- Recommendation: review the discrepancy before professional use

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Keep the type of statement visible.

The fact is directly present in Source A. The disagreement is derived by comparing A and B. The recommendation is a proposed next action, not a sentence copied from either source. If an organization has an official procedure, that approved procedure should replace a generic recommendation.

## Slide 17 — Detect the unsupported claim

- **Identifier:** S04-17
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Flawed draft: “SB-104 weighed 681 kg and was approved after correction.”
- 681 kg appears only in Source B
- “Approved” and “after correction” are unsupported
- The weight conflict has been hidden

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A sentence can contain one sourced value while still being unacceptable overall.

Have learners classify each part. Source B contains 681 kg, but A contains 618 kg, so presenting 681 kg alone hides the conflict. Neither “approved” nor “after correction” appears in the supplied material. This draft is deliberately wrong for teaching.

## Slide 18 — Correct the specific failure

- **Identifier:** S04-18
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Both sources identify SB-104 on 3 October 2026
- Source A says 618 kg; Source B says 681 kg
- The supplied information does not establish which value is correct
- Supported and limited beats fluent and invented

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Correct the actual evidence failure instead of asking vaguely for a “better” answer.

The revised wording preserves the shared facts, exposes both weights, and keeps the unresolved issue unresolved. Emphasize accuracy before fluency: an explicit limitation is professionally preferable to an invented resolution.

## Slide 20 — More input does not mean more certainty

- **Identifier:** S04-20
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- What was extracted?
- What was omitted?
- What was inferred?
- What does the source actually support?

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** More modalities can create more opportunities for extraction and interpretation errors.

Use a document with a chart as the mental example. A generated summary may omit a label, misread a number, or infer a relationship the chart does not establish. Verification remains necessary even when the input looks rich.

## Slide 21 — Verification is a concrete checklist

- **Identifier:** S04-21
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Names, dates, and values
- Decisions and commitments
- Missing information and conflicts
- Unsupported claims and facts versus recommendations
- Responsible human reviewer

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** “Check the AI” is too vague to be a verification method.

Choose checks that fit the document. For a meeting summary, decisions, owners, and deadlines matter. For the cooperative records, IDs, dates, weights, gaps, and conflicts matter. The checklist should help a person inspect the output, not merely express caution.

## Slide 22 — Professional transfer: meeting notes

- **Identifier:** S04-22
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Confirmed decisions
- Assigned actions
- Owners
- Deadlines
- Absent information → not specified

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The same evidence discipline transfers to ordinary professional documents.

If the notes do not name an owner or deadline, the summary should say that it is not specified rather than supply a plausible person or date. Verify any names, dates, commitments, and attachments before a professional message is sent.

## Slide 23 — Professional transfer: reports and briefings

- **Identifier:** S04-23
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Reports
- Briefings
- Proposals
- Document comparisons
- Shorter must not silently become more certain

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Compression should reduce length, not increase certainty.

For reports and briefings, define the objective, audience, length, and approved sources. Preserve missing or conflicting evidence. A concise executive summary is useful only if it does not erase caveats that materially change the meaning.

## Slide 24 — Final source-grounded activity

- **Identifier:** S04-24
- **Duration:** 5 minutes

### On-Slide Content

1. List the confirmed facts
2. Identify the missing information
3. Identify the conflicting information
4. Write one derived observation
5. Flag any unsupported claim in the flawed draft
6. Name the item that requires human resolution

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The learner can now perform a bounded information task from source to verification.

Give learners the complete fictional package. Accept more than one wording, but require the factual boundaries to remain intact: no invented fact, missing stays missing, both weights remain visible, derived content is distinguishable, and no official decision is fabricated. The unresolved weight discrepancy requires an approved source or responsible person.
