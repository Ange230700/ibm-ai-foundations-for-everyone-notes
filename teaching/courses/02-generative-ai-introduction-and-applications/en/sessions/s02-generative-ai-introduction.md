## Session objective

Connect a professional need to a generative capability, choose an output format, inspect a
proposed result against a defined source, and assign final approval to a responsible person.

## Facilitation principles

1. Show one main idea per slide and keep detail in the teaching notes.
2. Identify the cooperative, its source material, and the simulated results as fictional.
3. Ask learners to challenge the deliberately wrong answer before showing the revision.
4. Keep actual member, payment, and inspection information out of public demonstrations.
5. Leave advanced prompt methods for S03 and product comparisons in the references.
6. Rehearse the delivery with a timer before releasing final teaching materials.

## Slide 02 — Four steps for using generation

- **Identifier:** S02-02
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### Slide Role

course-objectives

### On-Slide Content

- Distinguish generating, classifying, and forecasting
- Choose an output that fits the need
- Check facts, rights, and limitations
- Define the human decision

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Choose an output, produce a draft, and check whether it meets the need.

Introduce the four actions as things learners will do today. Ask which step is often missed between drafting and sharing. Return to human review at the end.

## Slide 03 — Start with the task, not the tool

- **Identifier:** S02-03
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Incomplete report? → classify
- Future collection volume? → estimate
- Notice to staff? → draft text

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Classification, forecasting, and generation answer different questions.

Ask for an output for each of the three needs. A system may support several tasks; the required output makes the distinction here. Return to the cooperative scenario.

## Slide 04 — Communicating a lot-intake instruction

- **Identifier:** S02-04

### On-Slide Content

- Fictional cooperative near Soubré
- Need: remind staff of intake-record fields
- Audiences: field agents, then member producers
- Starting point: a fictional instruction supplied for this demonstration

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The work begins with an operational need and an exercise source.

Introduce the fictional cooperative and the intake-record need. The exercise instruction is not a real procedure or legal requirement. Intermittent connectivity does not mean generation can work offline.

## Slide 05 — One record, two outputs

- **Identifier:** S02-05
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Input: a fictional intake record
- Output A: “needs completion” → classification
- Output B: explanation for staff → generation

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Flagging a missing field and drafting an explanation are different tasks.

Contrast the “needs completion” label with a notice drafted for staff. Ask learners what each output does. Neither output certifies a lot or replaces staff review.

## Slide 06 — What the model does during the session

- **Identifier:** S02-06
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Earlier training → model capability
- Exercise request → proposed output
- Human review → correction or rejection

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The model uses previously learned patterns and the supplied request to propose a draft.

Walk through earlier training, the exercise request, and human review. Ask whether a new prompt retrains the model; it does not. Internal rules must be supplied through an authorized process.

## Slide 07 — Seven types of generated output

- **Identifier:** S02-07
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Text, images, audio, video, code
- Synthetic data, virtual worlds
- For each family: what result should we examine?

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The course introduces seven content families; they do not all need a live demonstration.

Give one brief spoken example for each output family. Keep model architectures in the references. Synthetic data can look plausible without being an observed record.

## Slide 08 — A capability becomes useful in a workflow

- **Identifier:** S02-08
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Education: draft material → teacher
- Development: proposed code → tests
- Fictional cooperative: drafted notice → manager

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Generating text is a capability; preparing a team-reviewed document is an application.

Separate capability from application in each of the three sectors. Name the check for each output: teacher, tests, or cooperative manager. Claim no measured benefit for the fictional case.

## Slide 09 — One campaign, several formats

- **Identifier:** S02-09
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Field agent → text notice
- Internal training → visual or spoken briefing
- Technical team → tested HTML guide
- Same fictional instruction, different checks

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Choose the output format for the audience and the evidence required.

Read the audience, output, and verification matrix. Ask what a spoken briefing needs checked, then what a static HTML guide needs checked. A guide is not a deployed traceability system.

## Slide 10 — A limited, fictional instruction

- **Identifier:** S02-10
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Teaching simulation: record lot ID, date, and weight
- If a field is missing: mark “needs completion” and request staff review
- No payment, certification, or quality rule is supplied

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A short source makes it possible to detect facts added by a draft.

Read the three lines of the fictional instruction slowly. Ask what they do not say about payment, certification, or quality. Keep this source available through slides 11–14.

## Slide 11 — Ask for a staff notice

- **Identifier:** S02-11
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Teaching simulation: “Draft a 40-word notice for intake staff.”
- “Use only the instruction on the previous slide.”
- “If a rule is missing, say so; invent no thresholds.”

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Context and source material guide a first draft without guaranteeing accuracy.

Show a prompt screen clearly marked “teaching simulation.” Identify its task, audience, and source. Save advanced prompting methods for S03, and use no real member data.

## Slide 12 — A plausible sentence can be wrong

- **Identifier:** S02-12
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Deliberately wrong simulated output: “An incomplete lot will be certified within 24 hours.”
- Where does the instruction state this rule?
- Do not publish this draft.

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Fluent wording does not prove that a draft follows its source.

Allow a few seconds of silent reading. Ask: “Where does the source give a 24-hour certification deadline?” This sentence was invented for the exercise; it is not an observed output from the IBM course.

## Slide 13 — Three checks before use

- **Identifier:** S02-13
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Facts: does every claim come from the source?
- Use: does the message fit the audience?
- Authority: who can approve and share it?

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Review is part of the workflow, not an optional step after publication.

Ask who checks factual claims and who authorizes the notice. The invented certification in S02-12 fails the first check. Keep private member records out of the demonstration.

## Slide 14 — Return to the supplied facts

- **Identifier:** S02-14
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Exercise draft: “At intake, record the lot ID, date, and weight.”
- “If a field is missing, mark the record as needing completion and request review.”
- The procedure owner approves the notice before release

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Revising a draft means removing inventions, not just improving its style.

Compare each sentence with the source on S02-10. The revised exercise draft removes the invented deadline and certification. The procedure owner still has to approve it.

## Slide 15 — Same source, different wording

- **Identifier:** S02-15
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Staff member: data-entry instruction
- Member producer: accessible explanation of an incomplete record
- Check both versions against the source

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Rewriting or summarizing changes the form without adding new rules.

Invite learners to explain an incomplete record to a member producer. Do not add a deadline or financial consequence. If translating for real use, check tool support and review the result.

## Slide 16 — A visual can help or mislead

- **Identifier:** S02-16
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Fictional request: a poster about three intake fields
- Check labels, order, symbols, and rights
- No invented official seal or inspection result

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Treat a generated image as a communication draft that needs review.

Briefly distinguish generating an image from editing one. Check labels, order, symbols, and rights on a proposed poster. An attractive picture is not proof of an inspection.

## Slide 17 — What would you change on this poster?

- **Identifier:** S02-17
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Teaching simulation: fictional intake poster
- Check: lot ID, date, weight
- Spot: false certification or missing information

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Reviewing a visual also means checking its wording, implications, and context.

Show a purpose-made poster marked “teaching simulation.” Ask for one detail to keep and one to correct. Use no real producer photos, third-party logos, or fabricated compliance evidence.

## Slide 18 — Some outputs need different checks

- **Identifier:** S02-18
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Audio: language, pronunciation, consent, and rights
- Video: sequence, consistency, and provenance
- Synthetic data: utility and the risk of confusing it with observed data

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** The content type determines what must be checked before release.

Give one spoken use for audio and one for video; mention virtual worlds as the seventh family. Check consent, rights, and provenance before release. Label synthetic data explicitly.

## Slide 19 — Generated code is still a proposal

- **Identifier:** S02-19
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Fictional need: display a list of required fields
- Possible output: static HTML or code assistance
- Check behavior, security, and accessibility

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** Plausible code must be read, run, and tested before use.

Keep the example at the level of displaying required fields in a static page. Suggested code proves neither correct behavior nor security. The next slide separates technical from business checks.

## Slide 20 — The page loads; is it correct?

- **Identifier:** S02-20
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Teaching simulation: an HTML guide for three fields
- Technical check: the content displays and remains readable
- Business check: every sentence matches the fictional instruction

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A page that opens in a browser can still contain a wrong instruction.

Show a simulated screen or a local page prepared ahead of time. Check display, readability, and keyboard access where relevant, then compare its wording to the fictional instruction. Browser display does not authorize publication.

## Slide 21 — Produce content or pursue a goal

- **Identifier:** S02-21
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Generation: a draft for review
- Agentic system: potentially several steps and actions
- Set permissions and oversight before any action

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A generation request and a sequence of actions have different levels of autonomy.

Contrast a proposed draft with a system that takes several actions. Ask which permission would be required before a sensitive action. No automated system approves lots, payments, or certificates here.

## Slide 22 — Design a limited use case

- **Identifier:** S02-22
- **Duration:** 4 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Choose an audience: staff, member producers, or technical team
- Choose an output: text, visual, or static guide
- Name a fictional source, one risk, and a human reviewer

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A sound use case names its output and how a person will review it.

Allow two minutes for discussion and two for preparing an answer. Pairs choose an audience, output, risk, and human reviewer using S02-10 as the only business source. Solo learners can work individually.

## Slide 23 — Need, output, check

- **Identifier:** S02-23
- **Duration:** 3 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- What is the need?
- Which output fits?
- What fact will be checked, and by whom?

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A proposal is only as sound as its review and accountability.

Hear two brief proposals, about thirty seconds each. Restate need, output, review, and authorization. If time is tight, hear one and move the other to the closing discussion.

## Slide 24 — Test value without inventing results

- **Identifier:** S02-24
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Possible measures: accuracy, required corrections, preparation time
- Protect data and check rights before a trial
- Responsible team decides whether to use the output

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** A measured trial can assess a use case; a hypothesis is not an observed benefit.

Distinguish a possible measure from an observed result. Ask who would check facts, data protection, and rights before a trial. The responsible team decides whether to use or share the output.

## Slide 25 — Three questions to take away

- **Identifier:** S02-25
- **Duration:** 2 minutes

### On-Slide Content

- Is this classification, forecasting, or generation?
- Which source and output format fit?
- Which claim needs checking before release?

### Teaching Notes

**Key takeaway:** AI-produced output is ready for use only after an appropriate review.

Ask for spoken answers using the notice from S02-12: text generation, the fictional source on S02-10, and the invented certification claim. Clarify any confusion before closing.
